I build physical models that end up as software you can run.

I'm a second-year engineering student at CentraleSupélec, specialising in electrical engineering, and I'm joining McGill this fall to continue in that direction.

Most of my work starts from physics — a transmission line, a battery cell, a robot that has to stay on a track — and ends as a tool someone else can pick up. Control systems and signal processing are where I'm most at home. Space is where I'd like to end up, orbital debris in particular: the problem that pulled me into the sector in the first place.

Now: deploying an inventory management system at ArianeGroup, Vernon propulsion site. Python, REST API, and a stockroom that badly needed one.

Projects

2026 · EDF R&D Python · NumPy · Streamlit live demo

Cable fault detection

A reflectometry simulator that locates faults in electrical cables, in both the time and frequency domains. Around 1 % localization accuracy, in a browser interface built so that someone without a signal processing background can still read the result.
2026 · EDF R&D Python · NumPy

EV battery range model

From a pulse test on a single lithium-ion cell to the driving range of a full vehicle over the WLTP cycle. Equivalent circuit identification, pack sizing, and a sensitivity analysis that explains the remaining 8.5 % error.
2025 · Robotics bootcamp Python

Line-following robot

An autonomous robot that follows a track with a PID controller and plans its route with breadth-first search. Two weeks, from tuning gains on a bench to a robot that finishes the course.
