Team project · Behavioural safety

Reading the tension in a room without ever recognising a face.

Shops deal with three recurring problems , aggression, medical incidents and theft , and almost all of them are noticed too late, once things have already escalated. AuraSentinel watches for the signals that come before an incident, and buys trained staff a few seconds to act.

The problem

A study in Île-de-France found that 94 % of cashiers had suffered some form of external aggression; the retail sector logged over 33,000 recognised workplace accidents in a single year, and tens of thousands of shoplifting incidents on top of that. Existing tools focus almost entirely on theft , a suspicious gesture near a shelf. They say nothing about a fight breaking out, or someone collapsing in an aisle.

We set ourselves a harder brief: detect behavioural risk , tension, aggression, distress, a fall , in real time, and do it without turning the shop into a face-recognition panopticon. The privacy constraint was not an afterthought; it shaped the whole architecture.

0

Raw video or audio frames that leave the store. All anonymisation runs on the edge device before anything reaches the model, and federated learning shares only model weights.

Two signals, one score

AuraSentinel fuses a visual and an acoustic stream into a single tension score between 0 and 1. When the score crosses a threshold, a discreet alert is pushed to the staff device with the type of situation and its location. The two channels are deliberately independent: a shout with no movement, or agitated movement with no sound, should each be able to raise the score on their own.

The visual channel

YOLOv8 tracks each person across frames, and MediaPipe Pose reduces every one of them to a 33-point skeleton , no faces, no expressions, just joint coordinates. The visual scorer then reads tension out of posture and motion: raised arms, sudden speed, a torso leaning into someone else. Below, the prototype flags an altercation with a tension score of 0.9.

The acoustic channel

Audio is anonymised the moment it is captured. A non-reversible pitch and formant shift erases any recognisable voiceprint while keeping what actually matters for detection , energy, pitch variance, spectral flux, the acoustic shape of a raised or panicked voice. The model never hears the words or the speaker, only the tension in the sound.

The alert loop

Alerts land on a staff device, prioritised HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW with the aisle and a one-tap response. Staff can take charge of a situation (so two people don't converge on the same alert), mark it resolved, or escalate to emergency services directly. Crucially, they also rate whether the alert was accurate , and that judgement is what teaches the system.

Learning without collecting

Each store trains its own copy of the tension classifier on its own confirmed and rejected alerts, so no raw data leaves the node. A central server merges only the weight updates with federated averaging (FedAvg) into a global model that every store inherits. In our multi-store simulation, accuracy climbs from around 60 % to 78 % over five rounds , the loop works, even if the numbers are on synthetic feedback for now.

Taking the bias seriously

A system that flags "tension" can very easily learn to flag people , young men, certain clothing, certain accents , if the training data or the human labels are skewed. Rather than wave this away, we wrote a full evaluation of where bias enters (dataset, labelling, feedback loops, camera placement, model architecture) and what it does downstream: discriminatory alerts, over-policing, loss of trust, and real GDPR and anti-discrimination exposure for the retailer. The mitigation plan, diverse audited datasets, human override, fairness metrics across demographics, third-party audits, transparent scoring , is written up alongside the GDPR-compliant data clause in the report.

What it does not do

This is a proof of concept, and it is honest about it. The tension scorer relies on hand-tuned thresholds rather than a trained detector; the federated results run on synthetic feedback; and none of it has been evaluated in a live store. The privacy architecture is designed for GDPR compliance under Article 6(1)(f), but a real deployment would need a proper Data Protection Impact Assessment, on-site signage, and the fairness auditing described above actually carried out, not just planned.
