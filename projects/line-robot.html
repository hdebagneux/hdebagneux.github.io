Two weeks to make a robot stay on a line and know where it is going.

Two problems that sound like one. Staying on the track is continuous control; deciding which way to turn at a junction is discrete search. The robot has to do both at once, on the same hardware, in real time.

Staying on the line

The robot reads its offset from the line and has to correct it without oscillating. A proportional term alone overshoots and hunts around the track; adding derivative damping settles it; the integral term cleans up the steady offset that appears on long curves.

$$u(t) = K_p\,e(t) + K_i\int_0^t e(\tau)\,d\tau + K_d\,\frac{de(t)}{dt}$$

Three gains, and no formula that gives them. Tuning is where most of the time went — and where the difference between a robot that finishes the course and one that leaves it is decided.

Knowing where to go

The track is a graph: junctions are nodes, segments are edges. Finding the shortest route to a target is then breadth-first search, which explores level by level and therefore returns the path with the fewest segments first.
